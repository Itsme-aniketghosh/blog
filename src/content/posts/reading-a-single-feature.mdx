Most of the difficulty in mechanistic interpretability isn't the linear
algebra. It's deciding what counts as a feature in the first place.<SideNote>
This is the part that doesn't show up in the equations, and the part that
takes the most time in practice.</SideNote> But the arithmetic is worth doing
slowly, because it tells you exactly what you're assuming.

## The setup

Take a residual stream activation $x \in \mathbb{R}^d$ at some layer. The linear
representation hypothesis says features are directions: a feature $f$ is a unit
vector $w_f$, and the feature is "active" to the degree that

$$
a_f(x) = w_f^\top x
$$

is large. If we have a dictionary of features $W \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times d}$
with $n > d$, then the activations are approximately a sparse combination

$$
x \approx \sum_{i} a_i\, w_i, \qquad a \text{ sparse.}
$$

The whole game of a sparse autoencoder is recovering that $a$ from $x$ without
knowing $W$ in advance.

## Pulling one feature out

Suppose we already have a trained decoder $W_{\text{dec}}$. Reading feature $i$
out of a batch of activations is a single matrix multiply:

```python title="read_feature.py"
import torch

def feature_activations(x: torch.Tensor, W_enc: torch.Tensor,
                        b_enc: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
    """x: (batch, d_model) activations. Returns (batch, n_features)."""
    # ReLU keeps activations non-negative — features fire or they don't.
    return torch.relu(x @ W_enc.T + b_enc)

acts = feature_activations(residual_stream, W_enc, b_enc)
top = acts[:, feature_id].topk(20)   # the 20 tokens that fire this feature hardest
```

The `topk` is the interpretability step, not the matrix multiply: you read the
twenty tokens, and you decide whether they share something a human would call a
concept.<SideNote>If they don't, the "feature" is probably a chunk of
superposition you haven't disentangled — or your dictionary is too small.</SideNote>

## Why the arithmetic is the easy part

The equation $a_f(x) = w_f^\top x$ is honest about one thing: it assumes the
direction $w_f$ is *fixed*. Features that are contextual — that mean different
things in different positions — break that assumption quietly. The number still
comes out; it just doesn't mean what you think.

So the discipline is: do the arithmetic exactly, then spend your skepticism on
the labels. The math will never tell you your feature is real. Only the tokens
will.
